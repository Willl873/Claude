## AI Research-Bias Assessment — Read This Section First

**Information sufficiency: C-grade, and structurally different from every other company in this project.**

Before applying any standard framework, one fact needs to be stated plainly: **Hyperliquid Strategies Inc. is not an operating company.** It is a "digital asset treasury" (DAT) vehicle — a corporate wrapper, created via a reverse merger between a defunct biotech (Sonnet BioTherapeutics) and a SPAC (Rorschach I LLC), whose entire purpose is to accumulate and hold **HYPE**, the native token of the Hyperliquid decentralized-exchange protocol. It has no products, no customers in the traditional sense, and effectively no operating business beyond managing a crypto balance sheet and buying back its own shares.

**This means the standard four-master framework (moat, DCF, competitive strategy) does not apply in its usual form.** The first-principles questions that actually matter for this kind of vehicle are:
1. What do you actually own when you buy PURR — and is it the same as owning HYPE directly?
2. Does the stock trade at a **premium or discount to the net asset value (NAV)** of its treasury holdings — and why would that gap exist or persist?
3. Is management diluting shareholders faster than it is accumulating value per share?
4. What is the *underlying* HYPE token actually worth, independent of the treasury-company wrapper?

**A significant data-quality problem must be disclosed up front**: sources gave conflicting numbers for shares outstanding. The most recent explicit figure found (150.8M fully diluted, dated 2026-02-03) is mathematically inconsistent with the currently reported market cap ($1.62B–$1.73B) at the current price ($8.25) — that combination implies **196–210M shares outstanding today**, roughly 30-40% more than the February figure. This is very likely explained by further equity issuance to fund additional HYPE purchases (the company was reported to be "seeking $1 billion" for further treasury growth) — but this was **not independently confirmed** in sources reviewed. This gap is disclosed transparently below rather than resolved with a guess, consistent with the "leave it blank rather than fake certainty" principle for C-grade names.

## 🟢 business-analyst (Duan Yongping lens) — Two Businesses to Understand, Not One

**Layer 1: What does Hyperliquid (the protocol) actually do?** Hyperliquid is a decentralized perpetual-futures exchange, founded by Jeff Yan in 2023, built by a notably small team (~11 core contributors), and bootstrapped without VC funding. Its governance is unusually clean by crypto standards: founder Jeff Yan's stated manifesto is "No investors. No paid market makers. No fees to the dev team. No insiders" — 31% of HYPE's total supply was distributed directly to users with **zero allocation to VCs, exchanges, or market makers**, a genuinely differentiated structure versus most venture-backed token launches. The protocol has crossed **$1B in cumulative fee revenue** and runs an aggressive buyback mechanism (99% of fees go to a fund that buys HYPE on the open market daily), which has reportedly reduced float faster than Ethereum's EIP-1559 burn mechanism reduces ETH supply.

**Duan Yongping's test on the protocol itself**: does Hyperliquid have a real, differentiated business? The revenue and buyback mechanics suggest yes — this is a protocol with real usage and real fee generation, not a purely speculative token with no underlying activity.

**Layer 2: What does Hyperliquid Strategies Inc. (the stock, PURR) actually do?** It does not build, operate, or improve the Hyperliquid protocol. It is purely a **balance-sheet vehicle** — it raises capital (via equity issuance) and uses it to buy and hold HYPE, plus it runs its own separate share-buyback program. **The "business" is capital allocation into a single asset, nothing more.**

**Duan Yongping's test on the wrapper**: is holding PURR a "good business" the way holding equity in an operating company is? No — it's closer to holding a closed-end fund than a business. The relevant question is not "is this a good business" but "does this wrapper create or destroy value relative to just holding HYPE directly" — which is the financial-analyst section below.

## 🟡 industry-researcher (Munger lens) — The Digital Asset Treasury Landscape

**PURR is not a novel structure — it's a copy of a now-familiar playbook.** MicroStrategy (Bitcoin) pioneered the "public company as leveraged crypto proxy" model; since then, multiple single-asset treasury vehicles have launched for other tokens (e.g., Ethereum-focused and Solana-focused equivalents). PURR is Hyperliquid's version of this pattern, explicitly marketed as **"the largest publicly traded digital asset treasury focused on accumulating HYPE."**

**Competitive/comparative context**: the entire category's investment case rests on the same mechanism — **use public-market capital-raising ability (equity issuance) to accumulate more of the underlying token than a typical investor could easily buy directly, and let per-share token exposure compound.** This only works for shareholders if (a) the company can raise capital at a premium to NAV and deploy it accretively, and (b) management doesn't dilute faster than it accumulates.

**Munger's inversion — why might a smart, skeptical investor avoid this entire category?** Because the wrapper adds a layer of **equity-market risk on top of crypto-market risk** without clearly adding value: if the stock trades at a premium to NAV (plausible here per Scenario B), buyers are paying extra for something they could replicate more cheaply themselves; if it trades at a discount, the market is implicitly pricing in distrust of management's capital allocation or execution risk. **Either way, this is a bet on Jeff Yan/Hyperliquid protocol fundamentals plus a bet on David Schamis (Hyperliquid Strategies CEO) not destroying value through dilution — two separate risk layers stacked on top of each other.**

**On the underlying protocol's competitive position**: Hyperliquid operates in the competitive decentralized perpetuals exchange space, where it has captured meaningful market share and fee revenue, but faces ongoing competition from other perp DEXs and from centralized exchanges offering similar products with deeper liquidity in some cases. A full competitive teardown of the DEX landscape was not conducted in this pass and is flagged as a follow-up item.
