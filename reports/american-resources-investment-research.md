## Step 1: Business Nature Analysis — Duan Yongping's "Right Business"

**One-line business definition — and why this is unusually hard to state cleanly**: American Resources Corporation is, as of its FY2025 filings, best described as **a holding company in active transition**, currently consolidating essentially no operating revenue, holding cash/investments (~$72M) plus a disputed minority stake in a separately-capitalized rare-earth refining company (ReElement), while simultaneously working to divest/spin off its remaining legacy coal mining assets (American Carbon Corporation) for a separately-announced ~$280M consideration.

**This is fundamentally different from the exciting "critical minerals platform" narrative** that dominates search results and press coverage. The narrative describes ReElement's operations (weekly rare-earth-oxide shipments, 16,000+ tonne annual capacity, a reported $1.4B government deal, a $150M private valuation round) — **but ReElement's operating results are explicitly excluded from AREC's own continuing-operations financials as discontinued operations.** Whatever ReElement achieves operationally does not directly flow through to AREC's income statement going forward, only through AREC's minority equity stake (at a disputed percentage) and any future dividends, sale proceeds, or fair-value gains on that stake.

**Duan Yongping's test — what's good about this business, in one sentence?**
> "AREC used to be a coal company; today, after two major spin-offs (rare earth and, pending, coal itself), it may functionally be closer to a cash-plus-minority-stakes holding vehicle than an operating business — and the honest answer to 'what does AREC actually do now' is genuinely unclear from public filings alone."
