## 🟢 business-analyst (Duan Yongping lens)

**One-line business model**: Moog designs, manufactures, and integrates precision motion and fluid controls for aerospace, defense, and industrial customers, across four segments (Space and Defense, Military Aircraft, Commercial Aircraft, Industrial) — the majority of revenue now comes from Space and Defense.

**This is a "picks and shovels" business for aerospace/defense platforms**: Moog doesn't build the missile, aircraft, or spacecraft — it builds the precision actuation and control components that make those platforms function, a position with genuinely high switching costs once designed into a long-lifecycle defense or aerospace program.

**Growth is currently strong and broad-based**: +13% revenue, +47% net income, and a record $3.3B backlog (+33% YoY) all point to a business executing well into a real demand tailwind, not manufacturing growth through one-off items.

**Duan Yongping's test — is this a good business, one sentence?**
> "Moog sells the precision parts that go inside things governments and airlines can't easily switch suppliers for mid-program — that's a genuinely sticky position, and right now it's riding both a defense-spending supercycle and a commercial aerospace recovery at the same time."

## 🟡 industry-researcher (Munger lens)

**The sector tailwind is real and substantial**: NATO members are targeting a rise in defense spending from 2% to 3.5% of GDP by 2035; a proposed U.S. FY2027 defense budget of ~$1.5 trillion would represent roughly a 50% increase over current funding; and management specifically cited potential 2-4x increases in missile production rates over the coming years tied to Middle East conflict-driven demand. **This is not a Moog-specific story — it is a sector-wide re-rating opportunity**, and the same tailwind applies to Curtiss-Wright, Woodward, Parker Hannifin, and other aerospace/defense component suppliers.

**This raises the obvious question the valuation gap poses**: if the tailwind is sector-wide, why does Moog specifically command such a premium multiple over peers exposed to the same tailwind? Plausible (unconfirmed) explanations include: smaller size attracting a scarcity premium, a stronger recent growth print specifically, or the market pricing in Moog's Space and Defense segment mix (+16% segment growth) more richly than peers' comparable exposures — **none of these were independently confirmed in sources reviewed, and the gap remains a genuine open question.**
